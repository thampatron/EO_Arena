Which open EO sensors can a benchmark actually use?

Every row below is checked against the operator's own documentation or an official data catalogue (Copernicus, USGS, NASA DAACs, JAXA, EUMETSAT, Earth Engine, Planetary Computer, AWS Open Data). Facts that could not be confirmed from a fetched source are marked UNVERIFIED. "Open" here means the imagery can be obtained at no cost; the licence tier says what a public benchmark may do with it.

Within each sensor type, rows are ordered by (1) how widely they are used in the audited GeoFM benchmark suites (PANGAEA, GEO-Bench, GEO-Bench-2, Copernicus-Bench, SustainBench, FoMo-Bench, PhilEO, SatlasPretrain, and the Galileo, AnySat, OmniSat and TerraMind evaluations), then (2) licence tier, then (3) coverage, from global to local. Sensor types are ordered the same way.

Open public domain, CC BY, Copernicus or NASA open policy: redistributable Free + terms no cost, registration or agency terms (e.g. JAXA, DLR) Non-commercial research use only; redistribution limited Not open commercial, quota, or government-only access

Free, open and non-commercial are different things

"Free" is about price. "Open" and "non-commercial" are about what the licence lets you do after you have the data. For a public benchmark the second question decides whether we can host, redistribute and let companies evaluate on a task.

OpenAnyone may use, modify and redistribute, for any purpose, including commercial.

At most requires attribution (CC BY) or share-alike (CC BY-SA, ODbL). Public domain, CC0, CC BY, the Copernicus and NASA data policies. This is the Open Knowledge Foundation's "Open Definition" sense of open.

Free + termsNo cost, but you accept the provider's own terms.

Usually registration, attribution and limits on redistribution. EnMAP (©DLR, no redistribution of editable data), JAXA mosaics, Bhoonidhi. Free to obtain is not the same as free to republish: each needs a licence check before we host derived tasks.

Non-commercialFree and often redistributable, but commercial use is forbidden.

CC BY-NC and CC BY-NC-SA, PRISMA, NICFI, STARCOP, MARS-S2L, FABDEM. Not "open" under the Open Definition. A leaderboard where companies (Google, IBM, commercial labs) submit models is arguably commercial use, so NC tasks may need a separate track or explicit permission.

Not openPaid, quota-based, or restricted to named user groups.

Commercial SAR and optical, NASA CSDA (US-government-funded users), Norway orthophotos. Cannot be part of a public benchmark without a data agreement.

Share-alike (SA) matters too: a task built from CC BY-SA or ODbL data must be released under the same licence.

What the audit changes for the taxonomy

  1. Licence tier decides what a public benchmark can host. Only the green tier can be redistributed without conditions. Hyperspectral (PRISMA, EnMAP), the methane label sets (STARCOP, MARS-S2L, Carbon Mapper) and the bare-earth and TanDEM-X DEMs are all non-commercial or bespoke-licence. Tasks built on them need a licence field and possibly a separate hosting path.
  2. The core constellation changed in 2025–26, and time-series tasks will see it. Sentinel-1A retired on 29–30 Jun 2026 and Sentinel-1C and 1D now fly as the pair, back to a 6-day revisit after a 12-day period from Dec 2021 to Mar 2025. Sentinel-2C replaced 2A in Jan 2025. Landsat 7 was decommissioned on 4 Jun 2025. NISAR L-band science products became public in 2026.
  3. Several long records are ending now. Suomi NPP data delivery ends 1 Nov 2026 (the standard Black Marble nightlights product is built from it). Aqua MODIS was planned to end in Aug 2026. ASTER's thermal subsystem was switched off on 16 Jan 2026. The DMSP SSMIS microwave record is expected to end this month. MethaneSAT was lost on 20 Jun 2025.
  4. Open sub-metre imagery is wall-to-wall only in the US and parts of Europe. Exceptions exist (Uruguay at 0.32 m under an open licence; South Africa at 0.25 m, supplied free but with no formal licence found), and Satellogic EarthView gives 1 m CC BY samples worldwide. The free NICFI tropical basemaps ended their original phase in Jan 2025. Tasks that need very high resolution in the Global South therefore depend on non-open data.
  5. Imaging spectroscopy is open but not systematic. EnMAP and PRISMA are tasked, and EMIT, HISUI and DESIS follow the ISS orbit (roughly ±52°). There is no wall-to-wall hyperspectral archive, which limits where atmosphere and geology tasks can be sampled.
  6. The "2015 cutoff" coincides with Sentinel-2A (launched 23 Jun 2015). The Sentinel-2 half of HLS starts 28 Nov 2015. If the cutoff is meant as "tasks answerable with the current open constellation", this is its justification.
