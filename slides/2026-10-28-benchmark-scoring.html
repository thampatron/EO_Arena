Sherrie's GeoFM Benchmark Scoring applied to the published evidence: 14,600 scores from 52 papers, 825 model comparisons, 155 image-based benchmarks.

Live: Benchmark ranking · Evidence map · an explanation window behind every metric

The metric (note, Eq. 1–6)

Score = the share of the gap between models that is real, not seed noise

  • Spread: how far apart models are in one paper's comparison (τ²obs).
  • Seed noise: how much one model's score moves on a re-run (σ²).
  • Score = τ² / (τ² + σ²), with τ² = spread − noise. 1 = all real, 0.5 = half noise.
  • Top-half score (our addition): the same, among the better half of models only.

Worked example: GEO-Bench-2, PASTIS, fine-tuned, 18 models

Spread5.81 mIoU points
Seed noise0.69 points (5 seeds)
τ² = 5.81² − 0.69²/533.7
Score = 33.7 / (33.7 + 0.47)0.99

Finding 2

The score saturates. Among strong models, many benchmarks stop separating

Score (all models)Top-half score

Median top-half score 0.78 vs 0.94 for the full score; 25 of 46 datasets fall below 0.8. A benchmark's value depends on which models it must tell apart, which the note already says (Eq. 6 is conditional on the evaluated population).

4

Finding 3 · note Eq. 2, 9–12

Model performance has three dimensions, organised by sensor, and the leftover structure is per dataset

  • K = 3 shared factors: held-out prediction stops improving and the structure is stable under resampling.
  • What explains the task structure: sensor 31%, subdomain 16%, task type 12%, domain 8%, protocol 3%.
  • So a single model leaderboard fails, and we dropped it.
  • The note assumes leftover differences are independent across tasks (diagonal Ψ). They are not: mean |r| 0.35 vs 0.30 by chance, p < 0.005.
  • Within the same dataset 0.59, between datasets 0.33, within a paper 0.34. A fourth factor does not help.
  • The missing piece is a dataset-level interaction, not another shared capability.

For discussion

Four changes to the method, and why

ChangeWhy
Remove σ²/n, not σ², from the spreadPapers publish averages of n runs. Subtracting full σ² over-corrects and understates the score.
Estimate σ² per dataset, not per domainDomain explains 3% of noise, dataset 62%. A domain-level estimate carries almost no information.
Block-diagonal residual covariance by datasetResiduals correlate 0.59 within a dataset. The diagonal Ψ double-counts datasets with many comparisons in Eq. 8.
Add a cross-lab variance termPapers disagree by ~4× seed noise. σ² describes a reproducible paper, not a reproducible benchmark.

Also worth considering: a per-model σ² (39% of noise variation is between models in the same comparison).

Next steps

What we do next

  • Measure σ² where it is missing. Replicate plan ranks datasets by evidence at stake: MADOS, RESISC45, GEOID-Flood, CropTypeMapping, Nandi.
  • Controlled reproducibility check: re-run a few models on PASTIS and Sen1Floods11 with 5 seeds to split seed from implementation noise.
  • Estimate dataset ceilings: train on train + test, evaluate on test.
  • Revisit the launch tasks given Sen1Floods11.
  • Decide the paper's headline: full score or top-half score.
  • Viki reviews the benchmark mapping (15 judgement calls).
