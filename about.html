About

A shared map of what Earth observation benchmarks cover, built so gaps are visible and corrections are easy.

EOArena aims to benchmark geospatial foundation models on the tasks EO is actually needed for. This taxonomy is its first step. It starts from existing frameworks that define where EO matters (UN SDG indicators, GEOGLAM Essential Agricultural Variables, GCOS Essential Climate Variables, GEO BON Essential Biodiversity Variables, the Sendai Framework), then records which labelled datasets exist for each need and whether any benchmark tests them. The benchmark ranking and model ranking summarise the published evidence; where no labels exist, a planned expert arena will let domain experts compare model outputs directly.

Every fact was checked against the provider's documentation, the dataset paper or an official catalogue, and the riskiest claims (dates, licences, benchmark membership) were re-checked in a separate pass. Anything that could not be confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED. Facet tags are a first pass and are under review.

Filters

Every dataset is tagged under all 14 filters using a fixed list of values, so filtering never silently drops a dataset. The ability filter is a hypothesis: whether tasks sharing an ability rank models alike is something EOArena will test.

GeoFM evidence

The GeoFM evidence filter, the evidence panel on each dataset and the Benchmark ranking say how reliably published results separate geospatial foundation models on each dataset. Every reported number carries an i button with its exact definition and computation. The source is a meta-analysis workbook of 14,600 reported scores from 52 GeoFM papers (compiled by Viki Mancoridis, 2026, unpublished). This site publishes counts and summary statistics only, never the individual scores, which belong to the papers that reported them.

The scoring follows the lab's note GeoFM Benchmark Scoring (S. Wang, October 2026). A task is a dataset–protocol bundle: here, one paper's comparison of at least four GeoFMs on the same benchmark, protocol, metric and training fraction, since the paper is the finest available proxy for decoder, split and implementation. Only original results count; copied numbers, ablation variants, non-foundation baselines and training from scratch are dropped. For each task, the variance of model scores is the observed separation τ²obs. The replicate noise σ² of one evaluation is taken from the standard deviations papers print as mean ± sd, averaged over the models in the comparison when at least three report one; 18 papers do, covering 187 of 825 comparisons. Because a reported value is usually the mean of n replicates, the observed dispersion is τ² + σ²/n; the stable between-model variance is therefore τ² = τ²obs − σ²/n, with n the listed number of seeds, the count the paper states, or 3 when unstated (flagged as assumed; provisional comparisons use n = 1). The effective discriminative information is EDI = τ²/σ², and the score is τ²/(τ² + σ²), the share of the observed spread that is repeatable model difference in one standard evaluation. The frontier score repeats the calculation on the leading half of each comparison (at least four models), to show whether a benchmark still separates the strongest models once weak baselines are set aside; it is an addition to the note, motivated by the full score saturating near 1 whenever a comparison includes weak models. A dataset's score is the model-count-weighted mean over its measured comparisons; where none is measured, σ² is plugged in from the same dataset under the same protocol family, or from the median seed noise of that protocol family, and the score is marked provisional. Intervals bootstrap the models within each comparison.

A latent-factor regression, ymt = μt + λtᵀθm + ηmt, is fitted to all imagery comparisons by alternating ridge least squares after standardising each task. The number of shared capability factors K is the smallest whose held-out prediction error is within one standard error of the best, stepped down until the task geometry is stable under bootstrap resampling of models. From this fit, dataset-specific signal is the share of a task's stable model differences that the shared factors do not explain (a diagnostic, not a penalty), and info share is the Shapley share of log det(I + ΛᵀC⁻¹Λ), the redundancy-adjusted information a dataset adds about shared capabilities, so near-duplicate datasets split credit. The fit statistics (cross-validation table, stability, how much of the geometry is associated with domain, sensor, task type and protocol) are recorded in data/evidence.json under latent.

Variants. The note leaves a few choices open, so every dataset and every dataset × protocol family is scored under each of them and the pages carry an Analysis settings strip to switch: σ² as the median (main) or mean of the per-model seed variances; self-reported scores kept (main) or dropped before anything is computed; the full-comparison score (main) or the frontier score as the headline; the dataset or the dataset × protocol family as the unit; ridge 10 (main, stable geometry) or ridge 3 (better held-out prediction) for the latent-factor diagnostics. The choice follows the reader between pages and is written into the URL. A visible note marks any non-default view.

Residual correlations. The note's identifiability convention (Eq. 2) takes the stable residual interactions γ as uncorrelated across tasks. This is tested: for every pair of tasks with at least six models in common, the correlation of the fitted residuals, against a permutation null that shuffles each task's residuals across its models. The mean absolute correlation, the share of pairs above 0.5 and the top eigenvalue of the residual correlation matrix are compared with the null at K and at K + 1; the numbers are in data/evidence.json under latent.residual_check. At K = 3 the mean absolute residual correlation is 0.35 against a null of 0.30, and K = 4 does not reduce it; the excess sits almost entirely between comparisons of the same dataset (0.59 within a dataset, 0.33 between datasets; within a sensor 0.42) and not within a paper (0.34). So what the diagonal convention misses is not a further shared capability but the stable dataset × model interaction γ that the note defines, spread over the several comparisons of one dataset: the appropriate fix is a residual covariance that is block-diagonal by dataset, not a larger K. The numbers, by paper, dataset and sensor, are under latent.residual_check in data/evidence.json.

Model capability profiles. The same fit yields θm for every model; the Model ranking shows these profiles (rotated to the principal axes of the task loadings, with each axis described by the datasets that load on it) and a general-ability scalar derived from them, fitted separately on independent evaluations and on all evaluations so the self-reporting effect is visible.

Where measured noise is missing. docs/replicate-plan.md lists the datasets whose score is provisional, ordered by the published evidence that rests on them, with the models to re-run at five seeds to measure σ² directly; it is the first thing EOArena's own evaluations should do.

Disagreement between datasets in how they rank models is not treated as a defect: it may be specialisation. What the score measures is whether a dataset separates models reliably under its own protocol. Because the evidence is a sparse table of single reported scores, the first-stage result is an evidence map rather than a definitive ranking; repeated, standardised evaluations on EOArena will replace the plug-in noise with measured values. The scripts scripts/geofm_scoring.py and scripts/build_evidence.py document the method and inputs.

Contribute

The data lives in plain JSON files in the GitHub repository. Report an error as an issue, or edit data/datasets.json and open a pull request. An automatic check rejects any tag outside the vocabulary. The contribution guide explains the record format and licence tiers.

Taxonomy data is released under CC BY 4.0 and code under MIT. Each listed dataset keeps its provider's licence; nothing here redistributes dataset imagery or labels.
