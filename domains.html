EOArena · Task 3.1 · domain audit · as of 29 Sep 2026

Which application domains can a GeoFM benchmark cover today?

For each of the 32 proposed subdomains: which frameworks name the need, which open sensors serve it, which open labelled datasets exist, and whether any existing GeoFM benchmark already includes it. Every entry comes from a source that was fetched and read; anything not confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED.

Datasets are ordered by (1) how many audited GeoFM benchmark suites use them, (2) licence tier, (3) coverage from global to local. Audited suites: PANGAEA, GEO-Bench, GEO-Bench-2, Copernicus-Bench, SustainBench, FoMo-Bench, PhilEO, SatlasPretrain, and the Galileo, AnySat, OmniSat and TerraMind evaluations.

What the audit shows

  1. Nearly half the taxonomy has no benchmark task. 14 of the 32 subdomains appear in none of the audited suites, and 7 more rest on one dataset or one suite. Oceans and coasts, cryosphere and solid Earth are essentially uncovered. Crop type and land cover are saturated: PASTIS appears in six suites, BigEarthNet in five.
  2. Many gaps can be closed by wrapping datasets that already exist. Openly licensed labelled datasets exist for glaciers (CaFFe, HKH), sea ice (AI4Arctic), InSAR (Hephaestus), methane (CH4Net, EMIT plumes), marine debris (MARIDA), mining (Maus) and utility-scale solar (Kruitwagen); non-commercial ones exist for landslides (CAS Landslide) and methane (MARS-S2L). None is in a benchmark suite except CaFFe (GEO-Bench-2). This is the cheapest contribution EOArena can make.
  3. Some subdomains have no open labelled data at all. These include agricultural management practices, rangeland condition, land degradation, snow, lake ice, soil moisture, minerals, informal settlements and electrification. For these the gap is the data, not the benchmark.
  4. Many "labels" are other models' outputs. Biomass (CCI, GEDI L4A), mangroves (Global Mangrove Watch), coral (Allen Coral Atlas, 60–90% overall accuracy), fire (MODIS/VIIRS active fire, MTBS), and coarse land cover (LC100, SEN12MS). A task built on them measures agreement with that product, not accuracy. The label-source tag in every table makes this visible.
  5. Open imagery and open labels rarely coincide for very-high-resolution tasks. Building damage, roads, vehicles and aircraft are available only on commercial imagery, mostly under non-commercial or academic licences (xBD, BRIGHT, xView, DOTA, SpaceNet). The largest methane sets are also non-commercial.
  6. Geographic concentration is strong. Crop type labels are mostly European (LPIS-derived). Fire labels are from the US and Australia, forest biomass from Finland, and xBD events mostly from the US.
  7. Several SDG indicators often cited for EO cannot be measured from imagery. These are 6.3.2 (in-situ only), 14.3.1, 14.4.1, 14.6.1 (policy implementation), 3.9.1, 9.4.1, 13.2.2 (inventories) and 11.5.1 (deaths). They should be dropped as anchors. 11.5.3 (damage to critical infrastructure) should be added.
  8. Resolution mismatch. Soil moisture, ocean colour and atmospheric composition are natively 300 m to tens of km, while GeoFMs are pretrained mostly at 10 m.
