Benchmark ranking

Which published benchmarks can tell GeoFMs apart? Score is the share of the gap between models that survives re-running with another seed; Reliable means at least 80% does and the noise was measured; Papers is how many labs reported it. How many seeds? Papers that print seed SDs use 5 (GEO-Bench v2, GeoCrossBench, EarthShift, TESSERA) or 10 (Prithvi-v2); EOArena's own protocol is 10 runs frozen-decoder and 5 runs fine-tuned (Tasks), with ties declared when 0.25 < P(A > B) < 0.75. Rule of thumb from the Seed noise column: to resolve a gap Δ between two models at ~95% confidence you need about k ≈ 2(2σ/Δ)² runs per model; PASTIS (σ ≈ 1.1 pt) needs ~10 runs per 1-pt gap and ~3 per 2-pt gap. The datasets whose noise is not yet measured, and the models to re-run, are listed in docs/replicate-plan.md.

How to read it

Why the columns are in this order. They follow the scoring note (S. Wang, GeoFM Benchmark Scoring, Oct 2026). The note's single summary per dataset is the bounded score τ²/(τ² + σ²) (Eq. 6), to be reported with its interval, so Verdict and Score (90% CI) come first; the verdict adds the one thing the score alone hides, whether σ² was measured or plugged in, which the note treats as the difference between a reliability assessment and an evidence map. Models and Papers follow because the note asks how far conclusions rest on sparsely supported entries: one paper means the reliability is within one lab's evaluation, so sort by Papers or read it next to the verdict. Spread (τ²obs, Eq. 10) and Seed noise (σ²) are the two inputs of the score, shown so a reader can check it. The Frontier score is our addition, after the note's quantities. More diagnostics holds what the note itself calls diagnostic rather than a quality measure: EDI (Eq. 4, unbounded, the score is its bounded transform), dataset-specific signal (Eq. 5), info share (Eq. 8, for suite design), plus comparisons, self-reported share and domain.

The scoring follows the lab's note GeoFM Benchmark Scoring (S. Wang, Oct 2026). More diagnostics adds the comparison count, EDI, dataset-specific signal, info share, self-reported share and domain, which are the note's diagnostic and suite-design quantities rather than the decision. A comparison is one paper's table with at least four GeoFMs on the same benchmark, protocol, metric and training fraction; a benchmark's row pools its comparisons, and the expandable detail lists them. Within a comparison the variance of model scores is the observed separation τ²obs; its square root is the spread column. Part of that variance is seed noise σ², the run-to-run variation of a single evaluation, taken from the standard deviations papers print as "mean ± sd" when at least three models in the comparison report one. The stable between-model variance is τ² = τ²obs − σ², the effective discriminative information is EDI = τ²/σ², and the score is EDI/(1 + EDI) = τ²/(τ² + σ²): the share of the observed spread that is repeatable model difference, between 0 and 1. Because a published value is usually the mean of n seeds, the observed dispersion is τ² + σ²/n, so τ² = τ²obs − σ²/n with n the number of replicates behind the value (listed seeds, the count the paper states, or 3 assumed). The interval is a bootstrap over the models in each comparison. The frontier score repeats the calculation on the leading half of each comparison, to show whether a benchmark still separates the strongest models once weak baselines are set aside; it is the answer to a score that saturates near 1 when a comparison includes weak models.

Where no paper prints a seed SD the score is provisional: σ² is plugged in from the same dataset under the same protocol family, or failing that from the median seed noise of that protocol family (about 1 point for both frozen and fine-tuned evaluations). Provisional scores say what the benchmark would score if its noise were typical; they are not an intrinsic measurement and are ranked after measured ones.

Dataset-specific signal comes from a latent-factor regression fitted to all imagery comparisons: ymt = μt + λtᵀθm + ηmt, with K shared capability factors chosen by held-out cross-validation and kept only if the task geometry is stable under bootstrap. It is the share of the stable model differences on this benchmark that the shared factors do not explain: high values mean the benchmark ranks models in its own way. It is a diagnostic, not a penalty. Info share is a redundancy-adjusted contribution: the Shapley share of log det(I + ΛᵀC⁻¹Λ), the information the whole suite gives about shared capabilities, so near-duplicate benchmarks split credit. Self-reported is the share of scores that come from the paper that introduced the model.

Source: a meta-analysis workbook of 14,600 reported scores from 52 GeoFM papers (Viki Mancoridis, 2026, unpublished), grouped into canonical datasets and placed in the EOArena taxonomy.
