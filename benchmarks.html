Benchmark ranking

Every benchmark dataset with published GeoFM results, scored by how reliably it separates models: the share of the observed model spread that is repeatable once evaluation noise is taken out (score = τ²/(τ² + σ²)). Click the i next to any column for its exact definition and how it is computed. Static, from papers; not a model leaderboard.

  • Reliable≥ 80% of the model spread is repeatable; seed noise measured from printed standard deviations.
  • Moderate50–80% of the spread is repeatable (measured noise).
  • Weak / saturatedUnder 50% of the spread exceeds measured seed noise: models are hard to tell apart here.
  • Provisional (high)≥ 80% repeatable, but no paper prints a seed SD, so noise is plugged in from similar comparisons.
  • Provisional (low)< 80% repeatable under plug-in noise.
  • Spread onlyNo noise estimate available; only the observed spread is known.
  • Under 4 modelsScored, but no single comparison has four models.

How to read it

The scoring follows the lab's note GeoFM Benchmark Scoring (S. Wang, Oct 2026). A comparison is one paper's table with at least four GeoFMs on the same benchmark, protocol, metric and training fraction; a benchmark's row pools its comparisons, and the expandable detail lists them. Within a comparison the variance of model scores is the observed separation τ²obs; its square root is the spread column. Part of that variance is seed noise σ², the run-to-run variation of a single evaluation, taken from the standard deviations papers print as "mean ± sd" when at least three models in the comparison report one. The stable between-model variance is τ² = τ²obs − σ², the effective discriminative information is EDI = τ²/σ², and the score is EDI/(1 + EDI) = τ²/(τ² + σ²): the share of the observed spread that is repeatable model difference, between 0 and 1. Because a published value is usually the mean of n seeds, the observed dispersion is τ² + σ²/n, so τ² = τ²obs − σ²/n with n the number of replicates behind the value (listed seeds, the count the paper states, or 3 assumed). The interval is a bootstrap over the models in each comparison. The frontier score repeats the calculation on the leading half of each comparison, to show whether a benchmark still separates the strongest models once weak baselines are set aside; it is the answer to a score that saturates near 1 when a comparison includes weak models.

Where no paper prints a seed SD the score is provisional: σ² is plugged in from the same dataset under the same protocol family, or failing that from the median seed noise of that protocol family (about 1 point for both frozen and fine-tuned evaluations). Provisional scores say what the benchmark would score if its noise were typical; they are not an intrinsic measurement and are ranked after measured ones.

Dataset-specific signal comes from a latent-factor regression fitted to all imagery comparisons: ymt = μt + λtᵀθm + ηmt, with K shared capability factors chosen by held-out cross-validation and kept only if the task geometry is stable under bootstrap. It is the share of the stable model differences on this benchmark that the shared factors do not explain: high values mean the benchmark ranks models in its own way. It is a diagnostic, not a penalty. Info share is a redundancy-adjusted contribution: the Shapley share of log det(I + ΛᵀC⁻¹Λ), the information the whole suite gives about shared capabilities, so near-duplicate benchmarks split credit. Self-reported is the share of scores that come from the paper that introduced the model.

Source: a meta-analysis workbook of 14,600 reported scores from 52 GeoFM papers (Viki Mancoridis, 2026, unpublished), grouped into canonical datasets and placed in the EOArena taxonomy. Original results only; copied numbers, ablation variants, non-foundation baselines and training from scratch are dropped. Benchmarks marked not in inventory have published scores but no record yet in the Explorer. The model ranking uses the same comparisons the other way round.
